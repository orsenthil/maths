A mind-reading number trick

The Vanishing Digit

A classic mental-math trick from Alexander Bogomolny's Rapid Arithmetic collection at Cut the Knot.

How to play

  1. Think of any 3-digit number — keep it to yourself.
  2. Write it backwards.
  3. Subtract the smaller of the two numbers from the larger.
  4. Below, tell me the first two digits of that result.

I'll guess the digit you left out — no calculator needed on my end.

How does this work?

Write your 3-digit number as digits a, b, c, so the number itself is 100a + 10b + c. Reversed, it's 100c + 10b + a. Subtract one from the other and the middle digit cancels out completely — you're left with exactly 99 × (a − c).

Since a and c are single digits, that difference between them is always somewhere from 1 to 9 (or 0, if you happened to pick a number whose first and last digit already matched). So the final result can only ever be one of these:

000, 099, 198, 297, 396, 495, 594, 693, 792, 891

Add up the digits of any of the nonzero ones — they all come to 18. That's the whole trick: once two digits are on the table, the third is just 18 minus their sum (and if you tell me "00", I already know the third digit has to be 0).

Reference: A. Bogomolny, Rapid Arithmetic, Cut the Knot.
