The Vanishing Digit

Pick a 3-digit number, reverse it, subtract the smaller from the larger — and somehow, seeing just two of the three digits in the answer is enough to know the third. Both of you race to call it first.

How to play

Hit "New round" to generate a hidden calculation. Two of its three digits show up on screen — the third is missing. Whoever buzzes in first gets one shot at the missing digit: guess right and the point is yours, guess wrong and it passes straight to your opponent. First to 5 points wins the match.

How does this work?

Write your 3-digit number as digits a, b, c, so the number itself is 100a + 10b + c. Reversed, it's 100c + 10b + a. Subtract one from the other and the middle digit cancels out completely — you're left with exactly 99 × (a − c).

Since a and c are single digits, that difference between them is always somewhere from 1 to 9. So the final result can only ever be one of nine numbers:

099, 198, 297, 396, 495, 594, 693, 792, 891

Add up the digits of any one of them — they all come to 18. That's the whole trick: once two digits are on the table, the third is just 18 minus their sum.

A classic reverse-and-subtract number trick — the kind of always-a-multiple-of-9 arithmetic that shows up throughout recreational mathematics.
